# Executive summary

## Decision and evidence
Prioritize a small fulfillment-efficiency pilot after validating costs with operating data. In this illustrative retail portfolio, a 10% picking-cost reduction plus a 5% handling-cost reduction yields $715.78 gross savings, the largest standalone scenario. Picking is the largest modeled cost-to-serve component ($5,332.80).

The combined strategy adds a 10% freight reduction and 15% shrink reduction to fulfillment efficiency. It yields $1,305.33 gross savings, $27,274.15 modeled contribution profit and a 20.42% margin (+0.98 percentage points). These are sample-period, hypothetical savings before implementation costs, not annualized or realized gains. Independent rounding can produce one-cent differences between displayed sums.

## Proposed pilot
Validate picking time, handling effort, freight charges and shrink with actual operating records. Select comparable pilot and comparison operations; establish a baseline over a consistent period and control for volume and product mix. Test process changes that reduce picking and handling effort. Track cost per qualifying line, handling cost per basket, service accuracy, stock availability and shrink. Record implementation and recurring costs; use net savings and service outcomes to decide whether to scale. No pilot has been conducted.

## Interpretation limits
The sample represents participating households, not complete store sales. The selected SKUs cover 38.25% of eligible retail revenue in the five stores. Procurement and operating costs are assumed; fixed overhead, delivery and payment processing are excluded. Contribution profit is not net profit. The analysis is independent and is not affiliated with DoorDash or based on its internal financial records.
